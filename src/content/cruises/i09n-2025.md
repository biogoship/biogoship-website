### Overview

GO-SHIP I09N 2025 ran aboard the R/V Thomas G. Thompson from Henderson (Fremantle), Western Australia, to Phuket, Thailand, under Chief Scientist Viviane Menezes and Co-Chief Scientist Leah Chomiak, with a Bangladesh Navy Naval Observer (Lt Cdr Sazzad Hossen) embedded for the first time to enable sampling in Bangladesh's EEZ at the line's northern end.

### Operations

Departure was delayed four days, from March 17 to March 21, when Australian customs held the DIC van in Singapore over trace acetone (a controlled substance in Australia); once cleared, the van crossed to Fremantle aboard the M/V Jogela and reached the ship just a day before sailing, trimming the planned station count from 122 to 106 before the cruise even began.

The first week at sea then delivered a cascade of equipment failures: both CTD test stations suffered winch-software freezes, unfiring bottles, and a tangled cable; the aft winch failed outright at Station 2, forcing a switch to the forward winch; a chronically noisy altimeter plagued repeated stations; Station 7's CTD failed on the way up with inconsistent bottle closures; and Station 8 saw a total system failure ("nothing functioned") compounded by a simultaneous failure of the ship's Z-drive propulsion, holding the vessel in place for nearly three days while Tropical Cyclone Courtney intensified nearby — the delays, ironically, kept the ship out of the storm's path.

The wire was ultimately found to be the culprit and re-terminated on the fourth attempt, after which operations stabilized; the reduced station plan bottomed out at 89 before recovering to 100 in Week 4 as fair weather, a functioning CTD, and a one-day cruise extension from the ship let the team claw back 12 stations, finishing with 98 completed (two skipped near the Bangladesh/Myanmar EEZ boundary to avoid a diplomatic complication, offset by nine entirely new stations sampled inside Bangladesh's EEZ compared to prior I09N occupations in 1995, 2007, and 2016).

### Scientific highlights

Along the way the cruise deployed Core and BGC Argo floats, SQUID floats, and Global Drifter Program surface drifters; crossed the Wyrtki Jets and the equator (marked with a seawater bucket-shower ceremony); and, in the Bay of Bengal, mapped the basin's pronounced oxygen minimum zone (oxygen near zero between the 26 and 27.3 kg/m3 neutral density surfaces, roughly 150-900 m) alongside a textbook salinity section showing the Subtropical Underwater salinity maximum, Antarctic Intermediate Water's fresh signature near 1000 m, and the fresher Indonesian Throughflow Water intermingling with it near 15°S.

### Fun facts

Styrofoam cups were dutifully crushed and sent down on casts exceeding 6,000 m; a small fishing boat strayed within feet of the CTD wire mid-cast, prompting an emergency recovery with bottles fired on the fly; the science party and crew squared off in a competitive Easter-basket-decorating contest; and the Chief Scientist briefly filled in as CTD watchstander during the equator-crossing ceremony before being relieved for a fresher set of eyes.
