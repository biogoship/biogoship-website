### Overview

GO-SHIP P15S runs along 170°W in the western Pacific aboard the R/V Investigator, operated by the CSIRO Marine National Facility (Australia), as part of the 2023–2030 decadal survey. The cruise sails in two legs:

- **Leg 1:** Lautoka, Fiji, to Auckland, New Zealand (September 29 – November 9, 2026)
- **Leg 2:** Auckland, New Zealand, to Hobart, Tasmania (November 14 – December 19, 2026)

The cruise also hosts the NF-POGO-SPC-CSIRO Shipboard Training program, and the section is a major contribution to the World Climate Research Programme's CLIVAR project and the Global Ocean Observing System (GOOS).

### Operations

Standard repeat hydrography along the 170°W meridian, spanning the Ross Sea, the Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC), and the South Pacific gyre, with 24-hour shifts of CTD/rosette sampling and underway measurements.

Operations include full-depth CTD/rosette casts, physical oceanography, carbon cycle parameters, and collaborative U.S. Bio-GO-SHIP sampling (including eDNA and biogeochemical measurements).

The map shows the track of the previous occupation of P15S in 2016, also aboard the R/V Investigator (expocode [096U20160426](https://cchdo.ucsd.edu/cruise/096U20160426)), which ran from 66.3°S to the equator.

### Planning information

**Data management and archival.** All core hydrographic data are processed and submitted to the [CCHDO](https://cchdo.ucsd.edu/) (CLIVAR and Carbon Hydrographic Data Office) following standard GO-SHIP guidelines.

**Logistics and compliance.**

- Strict biosecurity regulations apply (e.g., New Zealand biosecurity clearance, ISPM-15 compliant treated wood standards for shipping containers).
- All participating personnel must complete required onboarding profiles (such as UNOLS Marine Facilities Planning) and review the US GO-SHIP Code of Conduct.
- Mandatory pre-cruise training (such as joint Bystander Intervention Training) is coordinated ahead of field operations.
